# Spec - tb-pr-explainer

**Feature:** `tb-pr-explainer`
**Created:** 2026-11-18
**Design:** [design.md](design.md), human-approved through GateReeve
**Status:** proposed implementation requirements; implementation is not yet authorized.

## Summary

Replace the distributed explain-diff skill with pr-explainer. Given an identified code change and available supporting records, the skill generates a portable HTML explanation that prepares an experienced engineering reviewer unfamiliar with the changed area to examine the PR. It explains intent, resulting behavior, mechanism, existing evidence, and reviewer concerns without giving an approval verdict.

The first delivery includes the standalone skill, focused supporting resources, current naming/packaging updates, and a documented future GateReeve invocation contract. Mandatory artifact enforcement and improvements to product-test capture are follow-ups.

## Definition of Done

The current GateReeve full/supervised workflow applies. Acceptance uses the approved requirements, actual focused and broad verification, a fresh independent acceptance assessment, and a retained packet. These development gates are separate from the skill's product acceptance criteria. Merge, release, installation, and deployment require their own authorization.

## Acceptance Criteria

- **AC1. Replacement and portable invocation.** The native Codex and Claude packages expose pr-explainer in the canonical shared skills tree and no longer expose explain-diff as a skill or alias. Current caller/documentation/inventory references use the new name and its default artifact name, pr-explainer.html. The skill can explain a specified PR, branch comparison, commit range, or local change without requiring a GateReeve run. It resolves and reports the actual repository/comparison/revision or working-tree snapshot; an ambiguous or unavailable comparison is surfaced for resolution rather than silently replaced. Historical records and previously generated artifacts are not rewritten.

- **AC2. A coherent explanation for the agreed reader.** The HTML explains the relevant problem and context, documented or inferred intent, the PR's portion of the feature, resulting behavior, implementation mechanism, supporting evidence, and where the reviewer should focus. It opens with a clearly labeled, concise but substantive executive summary in plain language, giving the problem, meaningful change, high-level mechanism and scope, plus a material evidence limit or reviewer consideration where needed. The reader can understand the central story from that opening alone. The rest follows a logical reading order. It includes selected annotated code/diff excerpts with accurate links or references to the explained source. Consequential changes outside the excerpts are accounted for in the scope overview or reading guide. Missing design/plan/spec/issue material is identified without inventing it or preventing an otherwise honest explanation of the resolved change.

- **AC3. Useful visuals and meaningful before/after.** The skill chooses visuals appropriate to the change: captured product images where available and relevant, and source-grounded diagrams, structural comparisons, or state/sequence walkthroughs where they clarify the mechanism. Before/after presentations explain the same relevant behavior or structure; they do not imply that a conceptual diagram, baseline, or unrelated capture demonstrates an observed runtime outcome. Missing screenshots do not result in fabricated product screens or an avoidably prose-only explanation. Consider one or two overview diagrams in the executive summary when they provide the best orientation, without imposing a quota. Mermaid may be used throughout as an authoring format: render diagrams during generation and embed their renditions, preferably inline SVG, rather than requiring Mermaid source or a runtime at reading time. Interactive HTML is available when it clarifies the mechanism; controls do not hide the core summary. Apart from the required opening summary, structure is proportional to the change; a fixed section count, visual quota, simulation, and quiz are not mandatory. Quizzes are provided only when requested.

- **AC4. Deliberate discovery and qualification of existing visual evidence.** For visible behavior, the skill follows the relevant tests, Playwright/report configuration, and CI artifact-upload configuration to inspect useful existing captures, reports/attachments, evidence directories, PR attachments, and relevant run artifacts available through authorized access. It does not stop solely because default directories are absent and does not scan unrelated storage without purpose. Selected images are inspected and attributed to their test/run, revision, and scenario where known. Expected baselines, actual captures, and visual diffs are distinguished. Stale, inaccessible, expired, irrelevant, or unidentifiable captures are reported or clearly qualified, and are not presented as proof of the current candidate or an established before/after. Selected screenshots can be embedded; bulky traces/videos remain linked, with existing readable trace captures usable as attributed illustrations.

- **AC5. Honest evidence and reviewer concerns within the invocation boundary.** Ordinary invocation consumes existing source and evidence. It distinguishes tests added/changed, checks actually run, author-reported results, retained execution output, observed behavior, and missing or mismatched evidence. It does not run product tests, launch the application, capture fresh product-browser evidence, change tests/CI, fix product code, post to the PR, or perform delivery actions by default. Additional evidence collection requires an explicit request within existing permissions. Possible defects and spec mismatches are labeled reviewer concerns with source references and uncertainty as appropriate; no overall approval verdict or separate code-review process is introduced. Scores, metrics dashboards, and mandatory workflow enforcement are absent.

- **AC6. A usable, portable artifact.** The result is one self-contained HTML file with embedded styles/scripts and necessary selected images/visual assets; reading it does not require a server, external visual-explainer plugin, or remotely loaded rendering library. External source/raw-evidence links are clearly identifiable references. The page has a readable continuous main explanation, proportionate navigation and optional depth, readable code formatting, and keyboard-operable interactions with meaning conveyed beyond color alone. It renders without material clipping/overflow at desktop and phone widths. Actual artifact rendering and included interactions are checked when tools permit; unavailable checks are disclosed rather than claimed. An explicit destination is honored and artifacts for unrelated changes are not overwritten without request.

- **AC7. Governed inputs and a future integration contract.** When supplied a governed candidate, the skill uses the authoritative repository, base/head or snapshot identity, retained requirements, evidence references, and output destination without silently substituting a live branch, newer revision, or different feature home. The HTML and handoff identify the explained source, output path, unavailable inputs, and limitations. A documented caller/result contract explains how GateReeve can invoke the skill later, while current acceptance gates and authority remain unchanged. Any current artifact-name recognition needed for pr-explainer.html is updated narrowly; no new mandatory HTML gate, schema, or automatic PR publication is added.

## Evaluation cases and evidence boundaries

Use permitted read-only snapshots of real changes for the main explanation examples. At least one should concern visible behavior and one should concern non-UI behavior or structure. Existing records/screenshots can be reused with their actual provenance and limitations; do not rerun those applications merely to complete the skill demonstration.

Controlled evidence-discovery fixtures may supplement the real changes to exercise non-default paths, report attachments, missing records, and stale or unknown revisions reproducibly. Clearly identify fixture images and records as test material. Their discovery validates the skill's behavior, not the historical application's runtime. The expected-artifact list and expected source identities are validation inputs, not claims manufactured by the explanation itself.

Include an ordinary case without design/spec or runtime captures and a governed case with explicit base/head, available requirements, evidence references, and output destination. Cases may overlap where they exercise the intended behavior clearly. Artifact rendering/interaction checks are permitted checks of generated output; the default prohibition on new product-test execution remains intact.

## Explicit exclusions

- Keeping or aliasing the explain-diff skill.
- Mandatory GateReeve invocation or HTML passage conditions.
- Changing product tests, screenshot policies, or CI uploads to produce better captures.
- Review-performance metrics, confidence scores, dashboards, or a new evaluation platform.
- Automatically running product checks, fixing code, posting reviews, merging, releasing, installing, or deploying.
- Migrating or rewriting retained historical explanations and workflow records.

## Changes

Initial specification derived from the approved revised design and interview D1-D12. Amendment D13 incorporates the user-requested executive summary and explicitly permits rendered Mermaid renditions and appropriate interactive HTML; all other boundaries and exclusions remain.
